Forecasting Log-Oscillatory Dark Energy with ESA Euclid: Breaking the Degeneracy

Abstract:
Recent cosmological observations from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) Year 1 Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) measurements, combined with Type Ia supernovae and Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) distance priors, exhibit a mild preference (~1.5sigma - 1.9sigma) for dynamical dark energy over a cosmological constant. However, the standard Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) parameterization (w(a) = w_0 + w_a(1-a)) suffers from an unphysical monotonic plunge into deep phantom regimes at high redshifts, creating strong parameter degeneracies with alternative bounded models. In this work, we investigate a bounded, scale-invariant Logarithmic Oscillatory Dark Energy model, w(a) = -1 + A sin((2pi/lambda) ln a + phi_0), which possesses an exact closed-form analytical solution to the relativistic continuity equation. Utilizing the full 1580 x 1580 Pantheon+ systematic covariance matrix, DESI 2024 BAO, and Planck 2018 CMB priors, we find that current joint data favor both dynamical parameterizations similarly (Delta chi^2 = -3.75 for CPL, Delta chi^2 = -3.90 for Log-Oscillatory), resulting in overlapping 1-sigma posterior bands across z <= 2.3. We forecast the discriminating power of the ESA Euclid spectroscopic survey, demonstrating that Euclid's projected 1.3% fractional precision on H(z) over 0.9 < z < 1.8 will decisively break this degeneracy.
